The basics

What is NAS — and why do you need one?

NAS (Network Attached Storage) is a dedicated storage device connected to your home or office network. Think of it as your own private server — everyone on the network can store, access and share files from one organised location.

Unlike external drives that stay tied to a single computer, or cloud subscriptions that charge you monthly forever, a NAS gives you permanent, private, centralised storage — completely owned by you.

In plain terms: A NAS is a box on your network where your whole family or team stores files — photos, videos, documents, CCTV footage — all in one place, accessible from any device on the network.

How it works

One box. All your files. Always there.

A NAS plugs into your router and becomes a shared library accessible from any device on your network.

01

Connect to network

Plug the NAS into your router using an Ethernet cable — it becomes available to every device on your network.

02

Install WD Red drives

Slot WD Red NAS hard drives into the bays — purpose-built for 24/7 NAS workloads with RAID support.

03

Set up users & folders

Create shared folders for each family member or department. Control who can see and access what.

04

Start backup & access

All your devices automatically back up to the NAS. Access files from any device, anywhere on the network.
